### Brand narrative (reconstructed from multiple sources)
Momofuku restaurant group was founded in 2004 when David Chang opened Momofuku Noodle Bar in New York City's East Village. The restaurant was opened, in part, as Chang has disclosed, to stave off suicidal ideation — a personal detail he made public in his 2020 memoir "Eat a Peach."

### Chang's own framing of the origin story
- Trained in fine dining but wanted to break culinary rules
- Inspired by Japanese ramen bars, Korean food from his upbringing, and "ugly" food that tastes better than it looks
- The name Momofuku honors Momofuku Ando, inventor of instant ramen
- Positioned from the start as an anti-establishment fine dining disruptor

### Mission/philosophy signals
- "Ugly Delicious" — the name of his Netflix series captures the brand ethos: food that is visually imperfect but culinarily extraordinary
- Asian-American cuisine that doesn't apologize or over-explain itself
- Food as cultural connectivity and identity

### Awards and recognition
- Six James Beard Awards including Outstanding Chef
- GQ Man of the Year
- Time 100 honoree
- Momofuku Ko: Two Michelin stars (2009–2023, closed Nov 2023)
- Momofuku Noodle Bar: Michelin Bib Gourmand 2025

### Key business transitions noted
- Momofuku Ko closure (2023) signals retreat from ultra-fine dining
- Expansion into fast casual (Super Peach by Momofuku, Century City 2024)
- Growth of CPG goods business ($67M+ revenue 2024) outpacing restaurants as growth story
