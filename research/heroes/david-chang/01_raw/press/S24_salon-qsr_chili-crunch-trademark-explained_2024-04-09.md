## Overview

Salon's April 9, 2024 explainer on the chili crunch controversy provided a comprehensive account of the trademark dispute, community response, and its implications for Momofuku's brand identity.

## The Core Dispute

In March 2024, Momofuku — while attempting to trademark the term "chili crunch" with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office — sent cease-and-desist letters to at least seven companies that used "chili crunch" or "chile crunch" in their condiment names.

Most of the companies that received the letter were small, AAPI-owned businesses.

## Why This Was Especially Damaging

1. **Category history**: The chili crunch/crisp condiment has roots in Asian culinary tradition. Lao Gan Ma (Chinese brand) has been selling chili oil/crisp condiments since the late 1990s — decades before Momofuku's 2020 launch of Chili Crunch.

2. **AAPI community context**: Chang had built his brand on being a champion of Asian-American food culture. His restaurants, his media work, and his public identity all drew from Korean-American experience. Sending cease-and-desist letters to small AAPI condiment brands was perceived as a betrayal.

3. **IP vs. Generic term**: Legal analysts questioned whether "chili crunch" is generic enough to be untrademarkable — analogous to trying to trademark "hot sauce" or "salsa."

## The QSR Magazine Analysis

QSR Magazine's coverage (Inside Momofuku's Battle Over 'Chili Crunch' Trademark) provided trade publication context:
- Momofuku's trademark strategy was aggressive even by food industry standards
- The cease-and-desist campaign before the trademark was even granted was the specific point of outrage
- Industry observers noted the reputational risk was disproportionate to the business benefit of owning the term

## NBC New York Coverage

NBC New York's David Chang Momofuku chili crunch controversy explanation noted:
- At least seven brands received letters
- Several were very small, founder-owned businesses with limited legal resources to fight
- The disparity in resources made the power dynamic particularly uncomfortable

## Boston Globe: RI Chefs Respond

The Boston Globe gathered quotes from Rhode Island chefs responding to the controversy:
"No one should be able to trademark something so ingrained in a culture."

This quote encapsulates the community's core objection: "chili crunch" describes a category of condiment with deep cultural roots — it should not be ownable by any single company, especially one that entered the space only in 2020.

## The Apology and Reversal

April 12, 2024 (three days after the Salon explainer):
- Chang and CEO Marguerite Mariscal addressed the controversy on The Dave Chang Show podcast
- Confirmed they are no longer enforcing the trademark
- Chang: "I want to apologize to everyone in the AAPI community who's been hurt or feels like I marginalized them."

## Business Impact Assessment

The controversy did not appear to have a sustained negative financial impact:
- Momofuku Goods revenue 2024: $67M+ (record high)
- Retail locations by December 2024: 10,300 (record high)

This suggests either the controversy's negative impact was short-lived, that it also drove awareness (all press is press), or that the core consumer base remained loyal despite community outrage.

Alternatively, the controversy may have accelerated existing growth momentum — the chili crunch saga put Momofuku's products in front of millions of people who had never heard of the brand before the controversy.

## Lessons for Brand Management

The episode offers several lessons:
1. Trademark strategy must be integrated with brand strategy — legal moves have PR consequences
2. When brand identity is built on community solidarity, IP actions against that community carry asymmetric reputational risk
3. Speed of response and genuine apology can limit damage
4. Even major PR crises may not translate to sustained revenue decline if the underlying product is strong
