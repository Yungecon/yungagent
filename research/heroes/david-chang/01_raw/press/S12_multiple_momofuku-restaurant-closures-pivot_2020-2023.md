## Overview

Between 2020 and 2023, Momofuku systematically closed its most celebrated restaurant concepts, including the two-Michelin-starred Momofuku Ko and the pioneering Momofuku Ssäm Bar. This was not collapse — it was a deliberate strategic pivot toward consumer packaged goods.

## Restaurant Closures Timeline

**2020 (Pandemic Response):**
- Momofuku Nishi (Manhattan's Chelsea neighborhood): Permanently closed
- Momofuku CCDC (Washington, D.C.): Permanently closed

**2021:**
- Lease on original East Village Ssäm Bar space expired; restaurant relocated to Pier 17 at the Seaport in Lower Manhattan (a change that critics later cited as the beginning of the end)

**September 30, 2023:**
- **Momofuku Ssäm Bar closed** — "The new Ssam Bar felt like yet another expensive share plates spot—a trend it helped fuel—in a city with no shortage of them." The relocation to the Seaport had diluted what made the East Village original special.

**November 4, 2023:**
- **Momofuku Ko closed** — the two-Michelin-starred flagship, in operation for nearly 15 years. Ko had retained its two Michelin stars every year from 2009 until closure.

## The Strategic Rationale

Since Marguerite Zabar Mariscal became CEO in 2019, the company had been implementing a deliberate transformation:
1. Close high-rent, high-overhead fine-dining concepts
2. Retain scalable concepts (Noodle Bar, Fuku concessions)
3. Massively expand the Momofuku Goods CPG division
4. Raise capital specifically for the CPG business (~$30M in 2023)

The closures freed up operational bandwidth and management attention for the CPG growth push.

## Robb Report on Ssäm Bar's Legacy

"David Chang's Game-Changing Momofuku Ssäm Bar Is Closing This Month, Ending an Era of New York Dining." The article framed Ssäm Bar as a restaurant that "helped fuel" the entire share-plates restaurant format in America — an irony that the concept was being closed partly because that format had become oversaturated.

## What Remained Open

After the 2020–2023 closures, the surviving Momofuku restaurant operation included:
- Momofuku Noodle Bar (East Village, NYC)
- Momofuku Las Vegas (at The Cosmopolitan)
- Momofuku Toronto
- Fuku (concession locations, 30+ locations in stadiums/arenas)
- New openings: Super Peach by Momofuku (Century City, Los Angeles, 2025)

## Industry Context

The Lo Times Substack piece ("The Slow Collapse") represented a critical read of the closures as the end of an era for Chang's restaurant legacy. However, the financial data tells a different story: the CPG business that replaced these restaurants was growing at 585% over three years and reaching $67M by 2024 — suggesting the "collapse" was actually a profitable transformation.

## Reporter Gourmet Coverage

European coverage (Reporter Gourmet) framed the closures as the "End of an Era for David Chang," noting reasons including: changing dining culture post-pandemic, the economics of fine dining, and the strategic focus shift toward CPG.

## Key Quote (from search data)

Chang has stated that as he grew his CPG business, he spends less time on the restaurant side — implicitly confirming the deliberate deprioritization of restaurants in favor of the scaled consumer goods opportunity.
