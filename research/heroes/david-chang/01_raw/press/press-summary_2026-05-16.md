### 1. CPG pivot narrative — Momofuku Goods
**Sources:** Inc.com, Fast Company, Retail Dive, Tasteradio, Shopify Blog  
**Key facts:**
- 2023: $50M revenue, $17.5M Series A raised (Siddhi Capital + Alliance Consumer Growth)
- 2024: $67M+ revenue, 11,000+ retail doors (Target, Kroger, Costco, Wegmans)
- 2023 total company: $100M (restaurants + Goods equal split for first time)
- Goods = 50%+ of total revenue by end of 2023
- Feb 2025: Padraic Spence (veteran CPG executive) hired as CEO of Goods division
- Brick-and-mortar outpaced DTC for first time by Dec 2024
**YUNG frame:** This is the clearest example in food media of a chef deliberately building a revenue stream that doesn't require a lease. Chang didn't stumble into CPG — he hired a CEO in 2019 (Marguerite Zabar Mariscal) with explicit mandate to scale Goods while restaurants shrank.

### 2. Restaurant closures as strategic reallocation
**Sources:** Robb Report (x2), Eat This, Restaurant Hospitality, The Lot Times  
**Key facts:**
- Ssäm Bar closed Sep 30, 2023 (original location helped launch ramen-as-art-form in NYC)
- Ko (2 Michelin stars) closed Nov 4, 2023
- Prior closures: Nishi, CCDC (during COVID)
- Media framing: "collapse," "end of an era," "slow death"
- Business press framing: deliberate pivot
**YUNG frame:** The food press misread this completely. He didn't lose Ko — he freed the capital. Ssäm Bar's closure freed leases and labor hours. That capital went into the Goods division. The "collapse" narrative is what a food critic sees when they only watch the kitchen. The operator sees a portfolio rebalance.

### 3. Chili crunch trademark crisis (March–April 2024)
**Sources:** Washington Post, Today.com, ABC News, Buzzfeed, NBC New York  
**Key facts:**
- Momofuku sent 7 cease-and-desist letters to brands using "chili crunch" or "chile crunch"
- Most recipients were small Asian-American owned brands
- Public backlash was immediate and severe — boycott calls
- Chang responded via podcast (Apr 12, 2024) — The Dave Chang Show episode
- Momofuku dropped trademark enforcement
**YUNG frame:** Two lessons. (1) The podcast as crisis management tool — owned channel used to deliver a personal apology directly, no intermediary. (2) Brand risk of aggressive IP protection in a community-aligned category. The chili crunch identity had been built partly on cultural credibility; the cease-and-desist damaged that.

### 4. Majordomo Media founding + media architecture

**Key facts:**

- Lou Cona (ex-Condé Nast Media Group president) — Chief of Business + Revenue
- Scott Dadich (ex-Wired editor-in-chief) — creative direction via Godfrey Dadich Partners
- Christopher Chen — business partner

**YUNG frame:** He hired a media company president before he launched a single show. That's not creator behavior — that's media company behavior. Most food influencers become media figures by accident. Chang incorporated the architecture first. This is the lesson: you don't build distribution by accident. You hire for it.

### 5. Eat a Peach memoir + mental health disclosure
**Sources:** NPR, Goodreads, Audible, various review sites  
**Key facts:**
- Published September 8, 2020 (not 2021 as some sources note)
- Bipolar disorder disclosure — first major food media figure to go public
- Covers: Korean-American immigrant family, difficult father relationship, depression + anger, bipolar triggers
- Distributed via Random House + Audible + major retail
- Prism Magazine: "What not to do when starting a business" review angle
**YUNG frame:** The memoir did two things simultaneously: deepened the brand's personal dimension and opened a new audience (mental health community, business memoir readers). A vulnerability disclosure in a category where chefs perform toughness was a format experiment. It worked because it was real.
