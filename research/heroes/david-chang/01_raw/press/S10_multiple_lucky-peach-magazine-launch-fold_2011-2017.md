## Overview

Lucky Peach was a quarterly food magazine co-founded by David Chang, Peter Meehan (former New York Times food writer and Momofuku cookbook co-author), and Chris Ying, with Anthony Bourdain's media company Zero Point Zero Productions. Published by McSweeney's. Ran from 2011 to 2017 — 24–25 quarterly volumes.

## Format and Mission

Each issue focused on a single theme and explored that theme through essays, art, photography, and recipes. Themes ranged from conceptual ("The Apocalypse Issue") to geographic to ingredient-focused ("The Suburbs Issue").

The magazine was intentionally iconoclastic — the "vanguard, iconoclastic style has yet to be rivaled by a successor." It combined:
- Long-form food journalism and essays
- Art and photography
- Chef-authored pieces
- Cultural criticism
- Recipe content contextualized within essays

## Strategic Significance

Lucky Peach was David Chang's first owned-and-operated media property — predating Majordomo Media by seven years. It established:
- Chang's identity as a media entity, not just a restaurateur
- The editorial voice that would later define Ugly Delicious (culturally engaged, critically minded, anti-fine-dining-establishment)
- A community of food writers and intellectuals around the Momofuku brand
- A proof-of-concept for food-as-culture content

## Partnership with Zero Point Zero / Bourdain

The partnership with Anthony Bourdain's Zero Point Zero Productions was significant: Bourdain was the leading example of a chef who had successfully made the transition from kitchen to media personality. Lucky Peach can be read as Chang's attempt to build his own Bourdain-style intellectual media brand while keeping editorial control.

## Closure (2017)

Lucky Peach ceased publication in 2017. Reports indicated internal partnership disputes were a factor. Food52's retrospective described the magazine as having "a juggernaut of creative energy behind it" — the closure was widely mourned in the food media community.

The closure preceded the launch of Majordomo Media (2018), suggesting Chang absorbed lessons about editorial media ownership and pivoted toward video and audio (TV, podcast) rather than print.

## Legacy

Lucky Peach influenced a generation of food writers and editors. Its departure left a void in longform food media that has not been filled. The magazine is now collectible — complete sets are sold by Kitchen Arts & Letters and available on secondary markets.

## Issues

24 total volumes were published. Notable issues included the inaugural ramen-focused issue and Issue 23: The Suburbs Issue.

## Key Collaborators

- David Chang (founder, visionary)
