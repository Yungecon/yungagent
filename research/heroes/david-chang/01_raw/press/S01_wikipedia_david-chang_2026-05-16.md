## Early Life and Education

David Chang was born on August 5, 1977, in Arlington County, Virginia, to Korean immigrant parents. He grew up in Vienna, Virginia, alongside two older brothers and a sister. During his youth, Chang was a competitive golfer, participating in numerous junior tournaments, but quit the sport at age 13.

He attended Georgetown Preparatory School and later Trinity College in Hartford, Connecticut, where he majored in religious studies, graduating in 2000.

## Culinary Education and Early Career

After graduating, Chang enrolled at the French Culinary Institute in New York City, completing the six-month program while working the dinner shift as a cook at Mercer Kitchen.

Chang then moved to Craft, where he worked under chef Tom Colicchio, and later joined Café Boulud under executive chef Andrew Carmellini at Daniel Boulud's acclaimed French restaurant. He also spent time in Japan studying ramen, supporting himself by teaching English and working in various izakaya and ramen restaurants.

## Momofuku Empire: Timeline

**2004 — Founding:** With nearly $200,000 from his father, Chang opened Momofuku Noodle Bar in New York City's East Village, marking the inception of the Momofuku restaurant group. The name "Momofuku" is "an indirect nod" to Momofuku Ando, the Japanese-Taiwanese inventor of instant ramen. The star dish was his signature pork belly bun, which went on to be imitated by chefs and cooks across the country.

**2006 — Ssäm Bar:** Chang opened Momofuku Ssäm Bar a few blocks from Noodle Bar in August 2006.

**2007 — Momofuku Ko:** He opened Momofuku Ko, a 12-seat fine-dining tasting menu restaurant that takes first-come-first-serve online-only reservations exactly ten days in advance.

**2008 — Milk Bar:** Chang expanded into an adjacent space with Momofuku Ssäm Bar colleague Christina Tosi, launching Momofuku Milk Bar, famous for its whimsical desserts including cereal milk soft-serve and compost cookies.

**2009 — Michelin Stars:** Momofuku Ko was awarded two Michelin stars, which the restaurant retained each year until its closure in 2023.

**2009 — Cookbook:** Chang and former New York Times food writer Peter Meehan published the highly anticipated "Momofuku" cookbook, a New York Times bestseller.

**2010 — Midtown Expansion:** Chang opened Ma Peche in midtown Manhattan.

**2011 — International + Media:** He opened Momofuku Seiōbo in Sydney, Australia's Star City Casino redevelopment, earning three hats from the Sydney Morning Herald Good Food Guide. He co-founded the food magazine Lucky Peach with Peter Meehan and Chris Ying in 2011; it ran for 25 quarterly volumes until 2017.

**2012 — Toronto:** Momofuku opened in Toronto in a three-story glass cube in the heart of downtown, initially comprising Noodle Bar, Daisho, Shoto restaurants, and a bar called Nikai.

**2015 — Fuku Fast Casual:** In June 2015, Chang launched Fuku, a chain of fast-food restaurants specializing in spicy fried chicken sandwiches.

**2016 — Digital Delivery:** Chang launched his first digital-only restaurant, offering a menu only for delivery.

**2019 — New CEO:** Marguerite Zabar Mariscal was named CEO of Momofuku; the company began a strategic pivot closing restaurant concepts while expanding consumer packaged goods.

**2020 — Pandemic Closures:** Momofuku permanently closed Nishi in Manhattan's Chelsea neighborhood and Momofuku CCDC in Washington, D.C.

**2023 — Landmark Closures:** Momofuku Ssäm Bar closed September 30, 2023. Momofuku Ko closed November 4, 2023, after nearly 15 years in operation.

**2023 — CPG Fundraise:** Momofuku Goods raised nearly $30 million from Siddhi Capital and Alliance Consumer Growth for the consumer packaged goods division.

**2024 — $67M Revenue:** Momofuku Goods climbed past $67 million in annual revenue; brick-and-mortar sales outpaced direct-to-consumer revenue for the first time in December 2024; products available in 10,300 retail locations.

**2025 — Super Peach:** David Chang opens Super Peach by Momofuku in Century City, Los Angeles.

## Personal Life

David Chang is married to Grace Seo; they have two children. Chang has been open about his bipolar disorder diagnosis and struggles with affective dysregulation and depression. He published his memoir "Eat a Peach" in 2020, which he described as "a textbook on what not to do when starting a business."
