## Overview

During the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, Fuku — David Chang's fried chicken sandwich concept — closed its standalone restaurant locations but pivoted to a ghost kitchen / delivery-only model that enabled geographic expansion without physical real estate.

## The Pivot

**Pre-pandemic**: Fuku operated as brick-and-mortar fast-casual restaurants, including a Seaport location in Boston.

**2020**: Pandemic closures hit. The Seaport location closed. But instead of shuttering the brand, Momofuku/Fuku partnered with ghost kitchen platform **Reef Technologies** to operate delivery-only Fuku locations across multiple cities.

**2021**: Ghost kitchen expansion to Seattle, Dallas, Baltimore, and other cities. Delivery-only, available through DoorDash, Uber Eats, etc. Menu: chicken tenders, fried chicken sandwiches, waffle fries.

## Strategic Logic of Ghost Kitchens

The ghost kitchen model solved key unit economics problems:
- **No dining room**: No FOH labor costs
- **No prime real estate**: Reef's existing kitchen infrastructure in parking lots/urban locations
- **Rapid market test**: Enter a city without a lease commitment
- **Brand extension**: Reach new markets that couldn't support a full standalone restaurant

## Boston.com Framing

"David Chang's Fuku has shuttered — but there are plans for it to return in another form." The Boston.com article framed the closure as a temporary setback and the ghost kitchen pivot as the "return" in a different form.

## The Ghost Kitchen → Stadium Pivot

The ghost kitchen expansion proved the demand for Fuku's menu in non-NYC markets — but the model's limitations (reliance on third-party delivery platforms, thin margins on delivery orders) led to the next evolution: sports concessions.

By 2022–2023, Fuku had largely exited the ghost kitchen model in favor of stadium/arena concessions — which offered captive audiences, better unit economics, and premium pricing power without the delivery platform dependency.

## Significance for the Hero Study

The Fuku ghost kitchen episode is a textbook case of a hospitality brand using a crisis to test a new distribution model:
1. Pandemic forced closure of physical locations
2. Brand pivoted to ghost kitchen to maintain revenue and presence
3. Ghost kitchen proved market demand in new geographies
4. Learnings informed the next pivot: stadium concessions
5. Stadium concessions now the primary growth vehicle for Fuku

This three-stage evolution (standalone → ghost kitchen → stadium) demonstrates Chang's willingness to continuously reinvent the distribution model rather than defend the original format.
