## Overview

Momofuku CEO Marguerite Zabar Mariscal engineered the company's transformation from a restaurant empire into a diversified $100 million food and media business. This article examines how she led the strategic pivot that David Chang backed.

## Business Transformation Under Mariscal

Since becoming CEO in 2019, Mariscal has:
1. Overseen the deliberate closure of multiple restaurant concepts (Nishi, CCDC, Ssäm Bar, Ko)
2. Built the Momofuku Goods consumer packaged goods division into the company's primary revenue driver
3. Raised approximately $27.5–$30 million in growth capital for the CPG business
4. Expanded retail distribution from nearly zero to 10,000+ locations by end of 2024
5. Positioned the company to generate equal revenue from non-restaurant and restaurant ventures — approximately $100 million combined

## Strategic Logic

The restaurant industry has historically had razor-thin margins — often 3–5% net profit on revenue. Consumer packaged goods, especially at Momofuku's scale with established brand equity, can deliver significantly higher margins. The CPG pivot leveraged the brand recognition built over 20 years of restaurant operation and media presence without requiring the enormous fixed cost base of restaurant real estate and staff.

## The $100 Million Threshold

For the first time, Momofuku was projected to make as much money from its non-restaurant ventures as from its restaurants — approximately $100 million in combined annual revenue. The Goods division accounted for over half of total company revenue.

## Chang and Mariscal Partnership

The article contextualizes the relationship between Chang as founder/face and Mariscal as operational CEO. Chang has been open that he is less involved in day-to-day restaurant operations and more focused on media, brand building, and new concept development. Mariscal runs the business.

## Broader Context: Restaurateur-to-CPG Playbook

Momofuku represents an emerging playbook for celebrity chef brands: use restaurants to build brand equity and cultural credibility, then leverage that brand into higher-margin consumer goods that scale without the operational constraints of physical restaurants.
