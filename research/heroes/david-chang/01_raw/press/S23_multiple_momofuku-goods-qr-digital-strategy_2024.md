## Overview

In 2024, Momofuku Goods began a technology-forward initiative to use QR codes on packaging to create personalized digital experiences, collect first-party consumer data, and build a direct relationship with customers purchasing in brick-and-mortar retail.

## The Strategic Problem

As Momofuku Goods expanded from DTC (direct-to-consumer, primarily momofuku.com) to retail (Target, Kroger, Costco, etc.), it faced a fundamental data loss problem:
- DTC sales: Rich first-party data (purchase history, email, demographics)
- Retail sales: Zero customer data (the retailer owns the relationship)

At 10,300+ retail locations, the majority of Momofuku Goods sales were now happening through retail channels — meaning the company was losing visibility into who was buying its products.

## The QR Code Solution

Momofuku Goods placed QR codes on packaging that drove customers to:
- Personalized recipe content (seasonal, product-specific)
- New product launch information
- Consumer experience platforms

This created a "first-party data bridge" between retail shelf and Momofuku's owned digital properties.

## Capabilities Enabled

By collecting consumer data across different sales channels, QR codes allowed Momofuku to:
1. **Curate seasonal recipe content**: Personalized based on what product the customer bought
2. **Promote new product launches**: Target existing customers with new SKU announcements
3. **Adjust the overall consumer experience**: A/B test content and messaging with ease
4. **Build email/CRM lists**: Convert anonymous retail purchasers into known customers
5. **Understand consumer behavior**: Which products attract which types of consumers

## Business Context

This initiative coincided with:
- Amazon retail expansion (a key channel alongside physical retail)
- The transition to brick-and-mortar as the primary revenue channel (first time in December 2024)
- Preparations for the Padraic "Paddy" Spence CEO appointment to lead the Goods business

## Significance for Hero Study

The QR code data strategy reflects Momofuku's sophistication as a CPG operator — not just putting products on shelves, but building the consumer intelligence infrastructure that enables personalized marketing, product development insights, and customer retention.

This also reflects a broader trend in CPG: brands using packaging itself as a digital touchpoint to recapture the direct consumer relationship that retail channel partners control.

## Comparables

Other food brands using similar QR-to-CRM strategies include:
- Heinz (recipe QR codes on ketchup bottles)
- Several beverage brands using can QR codes for loyalty programs
- Momofuku's approach is more sophisticated in its data collection and personalization ambitions

## The Data Flywheel

If executed well, the QR → data → personalization → loyalty strategy creates a flywheel:
1. Customer buys Momofuku Goods in Target
2. Scans QR code → gets recipe → provides email
3. Momofuku now has a direct relationship with that customer
4. Can email them about new products, promotions, events
5. Customer buys direct on momofuku.com next time (higher margin, full data)
6. Or stays loyal to the Momofuku brand in retail

At 10,300 retail locations, even a 1% QR code scan rate on millions of units represents an enormous data acquisition opportunity.
